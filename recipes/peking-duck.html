Peking Duck

Description

This authentic homemade Peking duck features incredibly crispy, lacquered skin and succulent, flavorful meat, traditionally served wrapped in thin pancakes with hoisin sauce.

This recipe adapts the traditional Chinese restaurant technique for a home kitchen, focusing on achieving the signature glass-like, shattered-crisp skin and deeply savory meat. The process involves blithed scalding to tighten the skin, brushing on a sweet maltose and soy glaze to create a deep mahogany lacquer, and air-drying the bird overnight. It is slow-roasted to render the fat, resulting in an elegant, multi-textured masterpiece perfect for celebratory dinners.

Ingredients

    1 whole duck (about 5-6 lbs, thawed, innards removed)
    1 tbsp kosher salt
    1 tbsp Chinese five-spice powder
    6 slices fresh ginger
    3 stalks green onions (cut into halves)
    6 cups water
    1/2 cup Chinese rice wine (Shaoxing wine)
    1/4 cup honey
    3 tbsp maltose (or honey as a substitute)
    2 tbsp soy sauce
    1 tbsp white rice vinegar
    1 pack Mandarin pancakes (thin duck pancakes)
    1/2 cup hoisin sauce
    2 stalks green onions (white parts julienned)
    1 large English cucumber (seeded and julienned)

Steps

    Prep and season: Rinse the duck inside and out, then pat it completely dry with paper towels. Rub the inside cavity with the salt and Chinese five-spice powder. Stuff the ginger slices and green onions inside the cavity.
    Blanch the skin: Bring the water, rice wine, and honey to a rolling boil in a large pot. Hold the duck securely over the pot or a sink by its neck. Ladle the boiling liquid carefully all over the duck skin for 2 minutes until the skin tightens, shrinks, and turns pale. Pat completely dry.
    Glaze the duck: Melt the maltose, soy sauce, and rice vinegar together in a small saucepan over low heat until smooth. Brush this glaze evenly over every inch of the duck's tightened skin.
    Air-dry overnight: Place the duck uncovered on a wire rack set inside a baking sheet. Prop the cavity open with a wooden skewer to allow airflow. Refrigerate it for 12 to 24 hours. The skin must look dark, dry, and feel like parchment paper before roasting.
    Roast: Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C). Place the duck breast-side up on a roasting rack over a foil-lined pan filled with 1 cup of water (to prevent smoking). Roast for 60 minutes.
    Flip and crisp: Flip the duck breast-side down and roast for another 20 minutes. Flip it back breast-side up, turn the oven heat up to 400°F (200°C), and roast for a final 10 to 15 minutes until the skin is deeply bronzed and shatteringly crisp.
    Carve and serve: Let the duck rest for 15 minutes. Use a sharp knife to slice away the crispy skin first, then carve the meat. Smear hoisin sauce on a warm Mandarin pancake, top with skin, meat, julienned green onions, and cucumber, then roll it up to enjoy.
