Red braised Pork Belly

Description

This traditional Shanghai-style red braised pork belly (Hong Shao Rou) features melt-in-your-mouth chunks of savory meat coated in a thick, glossy, amber-red glaze.

This recipe highlights the classic Chinese "red cooking" technique, where pork belly is slow-simmered in a mixture of soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, and caramelized sugar. The slow braising transforms the layers of fat and skin into a luscious, gelatinous texture that loses all greasiness, while the meat absorbs the deep, savory umami flavors of star anise, ginger, and cinnamon. It results in a rich, comforting, and deeply satisfying dish that pairs beautifully with a warm bowl of rice.

Ingredients

  • 2 lbs skin-on pork belly, cut into 1-inch cubes
  • 2 tbsp cooking oil
  • 35g (approx. 1/4 cup) yellow rock sugar
  • 1/2 cup Shaoxing rice wine
  • 3 tbsp light soy sauce
  • 2 tbsp dark soy sauce (for that rich crimson color)
  • 4-5 slices fresh ginger
  • 2 stalks green onions, cut into 2-inch pieces
  • 2 whole star anise
  • 1 stick cinnamon or cassia bark
  • 2 bay leaves
  • Boiling water, as needed

Steps

  1. Parboil: Place the cubed pork belly into a pot filled with cold water. Bring it to a rolling boil over high heat and let it cook for 5 to 7 minutes. Skim off any gray foam or impurities that rise to the top. Drain the meat and rinse it under warm water to wash away residual residue. Pat dry.
  2. Caramelize the sugar: Heat 2 tablespoons of oil in a heavy-bottomed pot or wok over low heat. Add the rock sugar and stir continuously. Watch it closely; the sugar will melt, bubble, and turn a rich, amber-brown color.
  3. Coat the meat: Carefully add the parboiled pork belly chunks into the caramelized sugar. Stir-fry quickly for 1 to 2 minutes until every piece is evenly coated and turns a light golden-red.
  4. Build the braise: Pour in the Shaoxing rice wine to deglaze the pot. Add the light soy sauce, dark soy sauce, ginger slices, green onions, star anise, cinnamon stick, and bay leaves. Add enough boiling water to just submerge the pork.
  5. Slow simmer: Bring the liquid to a boil, then immediately turn the heat down to low. Cover tightly with a lid and let it simmer undisturbed for 1 hour, or until the pork fat feels incredibly tender when pierced with a fork.
  6. Reduce the sauce: Remove the lid and pick out the large aromatics (ginger, green onions, cinnamon, and star anise). Turn the heat up to medium-high. Cook for another 5 to 10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the liquid reduces into a thick, sticky glaze that hugs the pork.
  7. Serve: Transfer the glossy pork belly to a shallow bowl and serve immediately alongside hot steamed white rice.
